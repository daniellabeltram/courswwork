# GES Acquisition Targets: Top 10, Ranked

**Prepared by:** M&A Deal Lead (Corporate Development), using the Deal Sourcing Analyst's screening method and an Antitrust Counsel review of any target that competes with GES
**Date:** November 19, 2026

> **Please read first.** This report uses **public information only**. No one at any target has been contacted, and nothing in this report comes from confidential deal material. Every revenue, headcount, margin, multiple and price figure is marked **(estimate)** unless a cited source states it. Many company directories (ZoomInfo, LinkedIn ranges, CB Insights) are often out of date, so their figures are treated as estimates as well. This report supports decisions by GES executives and their licensed advisors. It is **not legal, tax, accounting or investment advice**. ## 1. Executive Summary

### Bottom line

GES should start outreach on its top five targets now: **Map Your Show** (event technology), **Marketing General Incorporated** (association membership marketing), **Condit Exhibits** (custom exhibits for Spiro), **Corcoran Expositions** (exhibit and sponsorship sales for associations) and **Orchid.Events** (convention housing for onPeak). All five are capability add-ons, each fills a gap Truelink has named, and each is small enough to close quickly. Each also has a public sign that it may be open to a sale: a private equity owner nearing the end of a typical hold, or a founder or long-time owner facing succession. Targets 6 to 10 add international reach (Saudi Arabia/UAE, UK, India) and freight logistics. They carry more antitrust, ownership or integration questions and should be worked in parallel at a slower pace.

**What we deliberately did not recommend:** buying regional general service contractors (GSCs). These are GES's direct competitors, so the antitrust risk is higher, and they add market share rather than a new capability.

### Analysis

**1. What we were solving for.** The question is which companies GES should approach first, given the playbook it has used under Truelink:

- **Capability add-ons, not market share.** 2Heads (creative agency, Dec 2025) and CSG Creative (association marketing, Nov 2026) both added services GES did not have (source: `docs/ges-company-research.md`, citing the PR Newswire releases).
- **Founder and brand retention.** Both deals kept their leaders and brands.
- **Tuck-in size.** Both deals were undisclosed and are likely "low tens of millions or less" (inference in the research file).
- **Truelink's three stated priorities:** (1) creative design, logistics and event production, (2) tech-enabled offerings, (3) international reach.

**2. How we screened.** We built a long list of **41 companies** across the seven priority categories. We checked ownership for each one using public sources, then removed any company already owned by Freeman, another strategic buyer, or a larger platform. That removed **16 companies** (listed in Section 4). We scored the remaining 25 candidates 1-5 on five tests, for a maximum of **25 points**:

| Test | What a 5 means |
|---|---|
| Capability gap filled | Adds a service GES does not offer today, or offers only weakly |
| Geography added | Opens a country or region where GES is thin |
| Cross-sell potential | GES can sell the target's service to its organizer and brand clients, and the reverse |
| Size fit | A tuck-in well below GES's ~$888M revenue (2023), likely under ~$50M enterprise value (estimate) |
| Culture and retention risk (scored inversely) | **5 = low risk**: an owner-manager likely to stay, with a clear succession reason to sell |

GES will not buy all ten. A realistic two-year program is **3-4 deals, or roughly $60M-$130M in total (estimate)**. That is about 1-2 deals a year, which matches the pace Truelink has set. These figures are not valuations. They are screening ranges meant to show whether a deal is "tuck-in size" or not. The `valuation-analyst` must build the real numbers after an NDA.

**5. Why buying beats building for the top five.**
- **Map Your Show:** Its floor-plan and exhibitor-portal software is already used by most of the largest US shows (company and directory claims). Building a rival product would take years and would still have to win those shows away.
- **MGI and Corcoran:** These businesses run on long client relationships, not on technology. Buying them is the only quick way to get the client list.
- **Condit:** It brings designers and a manufacturing base in a region where it already has clients.
- **Orchid:** It holds convention-bureau housing contracts that are awarded through relationships.

### Risks and red flags (ranked)

1. **High: integration capacity.** GES closed CSG only two weeks ago (Nov 5, 2026). Industry commentary says GES's deals are "won or lost the year after they close." Running more than two new integrations at once in 2027 is a real risk. **Mitigation:** sequence the deals, and use the stand-alone "collective" model (as with CSG) for the agencies.
2. **High: ownership facts are thin for several targets.** ArabExpo (unnamed "European parent" shareholders), Sho-Air (owner not found), Orchid (owners not named publicly) and Condit (headcount unknown) need ownership confirmed before any outreach.
3. **Medium: antitrust on the three targets that overlap with GES.** These are Full Circle (UK stand and shell-scheme contracting), ArabExpo (Gulf official contracting) and Orchid (convention housing alongside onPeak). None looks likely to block a deal. However, each needs counsel to check local market shares and filing rules (UK CMA, UAE, Saudi GAC) before any competitively sensitive data is exchanged. In the US, the 2026 Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) pre-merger filing threshold is **$133.9M** in deal value ([Covington, Jan 2026](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/ftc-announces-2026-update-of-jurisdictional-and-fee-thresholds-for-hsr-premerger-notification-filings)). On our estimates, only Map Your Show could come near it.
4. **Medium: channel conflict.** Map Your Show partners with GES competitors Shepard and Maritz ([MYS blog](https://blog.mapyourshow.com/blog/map-your-show-announces-partnership-with-shepard), [MYS blog](https://blog.mapyourshow.com/blog/map-your-show-announces-partnership-with-maritz)). Corcoran manages shows that award GSC contracts. If GES owns either one, rival contractors and some organizers may leave unless GES commits to neutrality.
5. **Medium: thesis drift into competing with GES's own CSG.** MGI overlaps partly with CSG Creative. The deal works only if MGI's direct-mail and membership-acquisition strength is complementary. That should be tested with CSG's founder, Paige Cardwell.
6. **Low: seller timing.** Full Circle completed a management buyout in 2024, so its new owners may not want to sell yet.

### What we still need

- **For every target:** audited or management revenue and EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization) for 2023-2026, normalized for show rotation; client concentration; and who owns the shares.
- **Map Your Show:** recurring vs. one-time revenue, client contract terms (termination for change of control), the Shepard and Maritz partnership terms, and Clovis Point's intended exit timing.
- **MGI:** gross vs. net revenue (direct mail includes postage and print pass-through), and a client list overlap check with CSG.
- **Corcoran / Orchid:** contract lengths with associations and convention bureaus (CVBs), and whether those contracts can be assigned to a new owner.
- **ArabExpo / Full Circle:** official-contractor appointments by venue and show, local market shares (for counsel), Saudi licensing and ownership rules, and the UK Companies House accounts for Full Circle.
- **Sho-Air:** who owns it, trucking assets vs. brokerage model, and safety record.
- **Specialists to involve next:** `trade-show-industry-expert` (validate the Map Your Show and Corcoran neutrality issue), `event-tech-expert` (Map Your Show product depth vs. Visit), `antitrust-counsel` (Full Circle, ArabExpo, Orchid), `valuation-analyst` (replace our ranges), `pe-operating-partner` (debt capacity and Truelink's appetite for a >$50M tech deal), `labor-relations-expert` (union exposure at Condit, Hamilton and Sho-Air).

### Confidence and assumptions

**Overall confidence: Medium** on the ranking. **Low** on every size and price figure.

- **Facts (sourced):** ownership events, dates, founders and leaders, and the client claims cited in each profile.
- **Assumptions (not facts):**
  - Revenue per employee: $150K-$300K depending on business type (estimate).
  - EBITDA margins: 5-30% by business type, benchmarked to Spiro's 9.7% margin in 2022 for exhibit houses ([Viad 2022 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884219/000115752323000233/a53313102_ex991.htm)).
  - Valuation multiples: 4-15x EBITDA, benchmarked to the ~7.5-8x Truelink paid for GES (inference in the research file). Smaller service businesses usually trade lower; software trades higher.
  - Our assessment that a sale signal exists is a judgment based on hold periods and succession, not knowledge of any seller's intent.
- Nothing public says any of these companies is for sale or in talks with GES.

## 5. Methodology and Sources

### Method

1. **Read the GES context:** `docs/ges-company-research.md` (business units, Truelink priorities, the 2Heads and CSG deals) and the role files for the Deal Lead, Deal Sourcing Analyst and Antitrust Counsel.
2. **Built a long list (41 companies)** across the seven priority categories from trade press (TSNN, Exhibit City News, Exhibitor, Exhibition News), deal databases (Mergr, PrivSource, CB Insights) and company sites.
3. **Checked ownership** for each candidate and removed anything already owned by Freeman, another strategic buyer, or a platform that makes it unavailable (16 removed). Recorded private equity hold periods (Map Your Show: Clovis Point since Jan 2022; Naylor: RLJ since Dec 2013; EventsAIR: Riverside since 2022; Cadmium: STG since 2020; Full Circle: Panoramic since 2024).
4. **Scored the rest 1-5** on capability gap, geography, cross-sell, size fit and culture/retention risk (5 = low risk). Ties were broken by category spread.
5. **Antitrust lens:** any target that competes with a GES business line (GSC, official contracting, housing) was flagged and rated Low / Medium / High. Regional GSCs were kept off the recommended list.
6. **Sizing:** revenue was estimated from headcount × revenue-per-employee, unless a source gave a figure. EV = estimated EBITDA × an assumed multiple. All are labelled estimates.

### Facts we could not verify

- Revenue and EBITDA for every target. None publishes financials. All size figures are estimates.
- ArabExpo's shareholders and its claimed Saudi show list.
- Who owns Sho-Air International and Orchid.Events (The Housing Connection, LLC).
- Condit Exhibits' current headcount.
- MGI's current revenue. The ZoomInfo $24.5M figure comes from an aggregator. The content of MGI's "new chapter" blog post could not be opened.
- Full Circle's revenue and exact headquarters. The British Business Bank page says Manchester; another trade article mentions an East Midlands MBO that may be a different company.
- Whether Cvent bought Bear Analytics. An aggregator says so; we found no primary announcement.
- The date of Connections Housing's purchase of Par Avion.
- Several sites (TSNN, Exhibit City News, MGI, IFES, ArabExpo, British Business Bank, Steelhead) could not be opened with our direct fetch tool. Their content came from search-result summaries or a secondary scraping tool, so wording should be re-checked against the original pages before external use.
