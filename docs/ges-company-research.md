# GES (Global Experience Specialists): Company Research

*Research compiled November 19, 2026. Written as background for building a mergers & acquisitions (M&A) application to support GES's acquisition strategy.*

> **How to read this:** Facts are drawn from GES's website (ges.com), press releases, SEC filings from GES's former parent (Viad Corp, now Pursuit), and trade press. Sources are listed at the end. Anything marked **(Inference)** is my analysis, not a reported fact.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

- **What GES does:** GES is a global exhibition, trade show and experiential marketing services company. It works for event **organizers** (as the official "general service contractor" that builds and runs the show floor) and for **exhibitors and brands** (booths, custom exhibits, brand activations).
- **Scale:** About **4,000 live events a year**, **150,000 exhibitors served a year**, work in **75+ countries**, and **2,600+ employees** plus thousands of union labor partners.
- **Ownership:** Private. **Truelink Capital**, a Los Angeles middle-market private equity firm, bought GES from Viad Corp for **$535 million**. The deal was announced on Oct 21, 2024 and closed on **Dec 31, 2024**.
- **Size:** On its last full year as a public segment (**2023**), GES had **$888.4M revenue** and **$68.2M Adjusted EBITDA** (about a 7.7% margin).
- **Acquisition mode:** GES is a classic **PE "platform" company** doing add-on acquisitions. It has closed **two add-ons in about 11 months**:
  1. **2Heads** (London experiential agency), Dec 2, 2025, folded into Spiro.
  2. **CSG Creative** (association and event marketing agency), Nov 5, 2026, bought from National Trade Productions.
- **Stated intent:** Truelink's managing partner said in November 2026: *"we continue to see a **robust pipeline of M&A opportunities** to build on the platform."* More deals are clearly expected.

## 3. The GES Collective: Business Units

GES calls its portfolio "The GES Collective." Each brand serves a different part of the live-events value chain:

| Brand | What it does | Customer |
|---|---|---|
| **GES Exhibitions** | General service contractor (GSC): show planning, floor plans, freight and logistics, material handling, booth furnishings, signage, labor, exhibitor services | Show organizers, associations, exhibitors |
| **Spiro** | Global experiential marketing agency and custom exhibit builder (the "official EAC", or exhibitor-appointed contractor, in the GES family). Brand concept: "Brand Gravity" | Corporate brands / marketers |
| **2Heads** *(acquired 2025)* | London-based design-led brand experience agency with offices in Los Angeles and Montreal. Now part of Spiro | Tech, financial services, consumer, aerospace brands |
| **CSG Creative** *(acquired 2026)* | Strategic marketing and creative agency for associations and events: attendee acquisition, membership growth, campaigns. Runs as a stand-alone operating company with its own brand | Associations, event organizers |

| **SHOWTECH** | Electrical and lighting services for events in Canada | Venues, organizers, exhibitors |
| **Visit** (visitcloud.com) | Event registration, data and event management platform (European leader) | Organizers |

## 5. The Truelink Era and Current Acquisition Strategy

### The sponsor: Truelink Capital
- Los Angeles-based **middle-market private equity** firm.
- Focus: **industrials, business services and technology-enabled services**.
- Target size: companies with about **$20M to $100M+ EBITDA** (for platforms).
- Approach: "operationally focused." It partners with management, **corporate sellers** (carve-outs like GES from Viad) and founders, and builds value through *"strategic initiatives and **transformative add-on acquisitions**."*
- Other recent deals: Johnson Controls' Global Intrusion Products business (carve-out) and JT Thorpe Group. Truelink won a Sagard "Emerging Manager of the Year" award in 2026.

### Stated value-creation priorities for GES (from Truelink at close)
Luke Myers, at close (Dec 2024):
1. Bolster **creative design, logistics and event production** strengths at GES and Spiro.
2. **Accelerate tech-enabled offerings.**
3. **Expand international reach.**

### Add-on #1: 2Heads (Dec 2025)
- **Target:** London creative/experiential agency, 40+ years old, offices in London, LA and Montreal.
- **Placement:** Folded into **Spiro**.
- **Rationale:** Grow Spiro's creative footprint in the UK and Europe and add strategic and digital capabilities.
- **Sell-side advisor:** PwC Corporate Finance (UK).
- **Terms:** Not disclosed.
- Myers: *"another meaningful step in advancing our vision for GES and its brands as the **premier end-to-end partner for live experiences worldwide**."*

### Add-on #2: CSG Creative (Nov 2026)
- **Target:** Association and event marketing agency (~25 years old), carved out from **National Trade Productions, Inc. (NTP)**, a show organizer.
- **Placement:** Stand-alone **operating company** under GES; keeps its brand, team and founder.
- **Rationale:** Moves GES **upstream**, from building the show floor to *filling it* with attendees and exhibitors. Gives two-way cross-sell: CSG services to GES organizer clients, and GES services to CSG's association clients.
- **Terms:** Not disclosed.
- Myers: *"Together, GES and CSG give associations and event organizers **a single partner from attendee marketing through show execution**, and we continue to see a **robust pipeline of M&A opportunities** to build on the platform."*

### Reading the pattern **(Inference)**
| Signal | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Both add-ons are **agencies / capability buys**, not GSC competitors | The near-term focus is **adding services** (creative, marketing, digital) that raise revenue per client, not buying market share |
| Both kept their **brands and founders** | GES uses a **"collective / house of brands"** integration model. Retaining people and clients matters more than full absorption |
| Deals are **small and undisclosed** | Typical tuck-ins (likely low tens of millions or less) that can close quickly and repeatedly |
| One deal was a **carve-out from an organizer** (NTP) | GES is open to buying non-core units from industry partners |
| Cadence of about **1 deal per year, speeding up** | A PE hold is typically 4–6 years (exit roughly 2029–2031). Expect the pace to pick up to build a bigger exit story |

### Likely target categories going forward **(Inference)**
1. **Experiential / creative agencies** in new regions (Europe, Middle East, APAC) to feed Spiro.
2. **Association and organizer services**: marketing, sponsorship sales, exhibitor sales outsourcing, content and education.
3. **Event technology**: registration, lead retrieval, floor-plan and booth-sales software, attendee data and analytics (extends Visit and the "tech-enabled" theme).
4. **Regional general service contractors and specialty contractors** (AV, electrical, rigging, furnishings, graphics, freight) to fill gaps in cities or add margin-rich services that are currently outsourced.
5. **Housing and travel** businesses that are bolt-ons to onPeak.
6. **International exhibition contractors** in growing markets: GCC/Saudi Arabia, India, Southeast Asia.

## 6. Industry and Competitive Landscape

### How the trade show value chain works
- **Show organizers / owners** (associations, Informa, RX, Emerald, Clarion, NTP, etc.) own the event.
- **Venues** (convention centers, Venetian Expo, etc.) host it.
- **General Service Contractor (GSC)**: the official contractor picked by the organizer. It handles floor layout, freight, material handling, furnishings, carpet, signage and exhibitor service kits. **This is GES's core role.**
- **Exhibitor-Appointed Contractors (EACs) / exhibit houses** design and build custom booths for individual exhibitors. **This is Spiro's role.**
- **Specialty services**: AV, electrical, housing, registration, lead retrieval, marketing.

### Key competitors

| Competitor | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Freeman** | GSC + agencies | Largest in the industry and GES's main rival. Family-controlled, with KKR financing behind its acquisition of the brand agency **Sparks** (2023). Has bought GSCs before (e.g., Metropolitan Expo Services) and sold **Fern Exposition**. |
| **Shepard** | GSC | Employee-owned, 120 years old, Atlanta/Las Vegas. Full GSC plus exhibits and corporate events. |
| **Fern Exposition Services** | GSC | Cincinnati. Spun out of Freeman; acquisitive itself (Triumph Expo, DWA, KiwiLive). |
| **Heritage** | GSC | Employee-owned, offices nationwide. |
| **Hargrove** | GSC / events | Washington, D.C. area. Trade show, convention and special events. |
| **The Expo Group** | GSC / experiential | Dallas. PE-backed (New State Capital, 2024). |
| **Brede / Alliance-type regional GSCs** | Regional GSC | Fragmented layer of mid-size regional players. **Natural consolidation targets.** |
| **Nth Degree** | Event labor / management | Majority owned by Shamrock Capital (2024). |
| **Exhibit houses**: Skyline (Gemspring Capital), MC² (MCH Group), Derse, Catalyst, Condit, MSM, Steelhead, Triple20, CDS (New State Capital) | EAC / exhibits | Compete with Spiro. **Many mid-size private houses are possible targets.** |

### Industry M&A climate
- **Private equity is consolidating live events.** Recent deals include Truelink–GES ($535M), Apollo–Venetian Expo (~$2.25B, 2021), Shamrock–Nth Degree (2024), New State Capital–The Expo Group and CDS, Gemspring–Skyline/TradeTec (2020), and Freeman–Sparks (2023).
- **Thesis:** Face-to-face events came back strongly after COVID. The market is **fragmented**, with many founder-owned agencies and regional contractors, and buyers want end-to-end "one partner" offerings.
- **Integration risk** is the main issue after a deal. Industry commentary on the CSG deal: *"deals are won or lost the year after they close."*

## 7. Strategic Considerations and Risks

| Area | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Labor** | Heavily unionized show-floor labor (thousands of union partners). Acquired contractors bring union contracts, jurisdictions and work-rule exposure |
| **Cyclicality** | Event revenue depends on corporate marketing budgets and the economy. COVID took GES revenue to roughly a third of normal in 2020–21 |
| **Show rotation** | Non-annual shows make year-over-year revenue lumpy, so targets need normalized financials |
| **Contract concentration** | GSC revenue depends on multi-year organizer contracts. **Change-of-control clauses** and renewal dates are key diligence items |
| **Culture and retention** | GES keeps founders and brands, so earn-outs, retention packages and key-person risk matter |
| **International** | FX exposure, local labor law, and data-privacy rules (GDPR for Visit/registration data) |
| **Leverage** | As a PE-owned company, GES likely carries acquisition debt. Deal sizing must fit covenant and lender limits **(Inference)** |
| **Data / cyber** | Registration and attendee data (Visit, onPeak) create privacy and breach liability, which is a diligence focus for tech targets |

## 8. Implications for the M&A Application

GES is a **serial acquirer** running a **buy-and-build** playbook under PE ownership with a "house of brands" integration model. An app that helps them should support the whole deal lifecycle, from **sourcing** through **integration**. Suggested scope **(Inference / product recommendations)**:

### A. Target sourcing and pipeline (deal funnel)
- A database of targets tagged by **category**: experiential agency, association marketing, event tech, regional GSC, specialty contractor (AV, electrical, rigging, graphics, freight), housing, international contractor.
- Fields: HQ and regions, headcount, estimated revenue/EBITDA, ownership (founder / PE / employee-owned / corporate carve-out), key clients and shows, union affiliations, website, contacts.
- **Pipeline stages:** Identified → Contacted → NDA → IOI → LOI → Diligence → Signed → Closed → Integrating, plus Kanban and list views.
- **Strategic-fit scoring** against GES's thesis: service-line gap filled, geography added, cross-sell potential, culture/retention risk, size fit.
- **Signal tracking:** founder age or succession, PE hold period of competitors' assets, organizer carve-outs (like NTP → CSG).

### B. Valuation and deal modeling
- A simple **EBITDA × multiple** model with scenarios. Use GES's own ~7.5–8x as a benchmark, and agency tuck-ins usually lower.
- **Normalization tools** for show rotation (non-annual events) and COVID-distorted years.
- **Earn-out and retention** structures (important given the "keep the founder" model).
- **Synergy model** split into cross-sell revenue (e.g., CSG ↔ GES organizer clients) and cost synergies (freight, warehousing, labor).

### C. Due diligence workspace
- Checklists tailored to event services: organizer **contract terms and change-of-control clauses**, **union agreements**, show calendar and renewal dates, client concentration, warehouse and equipment assets, data privacy (GDPR), and insurance and safety records.
- Document request lists, owner assignment, status tracking and red-flag logging.

### D. Integration (post-close, "the year after")
- **100-day plan** templates for the GES "collective" model: brand stays, back office integrates.
- Workstreams: finance, HR/benefits, IT/systems, sales cross-sell introductions, client communications, union transitions.
- **Synergy realization tracking** against the deal model, and **retention dashboards** for key people.

### E. Portfolio view and reporting
- A dashboard of all acquired companies (2Heads, CSG and future deals) showing their performance against the plan.
- Board and sponsor (Truelink) reporting: deal counts, capital deployed, pipeline value, synergy capture.

### Suggested core data model (starting point)
`Company/Target` · `Contact` · `Deal` (stage, owner, dates, valuation) · `ScoreCard` · `DiligenceItem` · `Document` · `IntegrationPlan` / `Workstream` / `Task` · `SynergyLine` · `PortfolioCompany` (post-close KPIs)

## 9. Open Questions to Validate with GES

1. What are the current **target criteria** (size, EBITDA range, geography, service lines)?
2. Who **owns the M&A process**: GES corporate development, Truelink deal team, or both? Who are the app's users?
3. What **systems** do they use today (CRM, data rooms, Excel models, DealCloud/Affinity)? What would the app replace or integrate with?
4. How do they **integrate today**: stand-alone brand (CSG) or folded in (2Heads → Spiro)? What decides which?
5. What **approval gates** and reporting does Truelink require (IC memos, board packs)?
6. Any confidentiality requirements, such as hosting, SSO and access controls for deal data?
